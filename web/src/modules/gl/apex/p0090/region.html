How the general ledger works

Account combinations, CCIDs, posting, reversal and balances, in seven short steps.

The account combination

Every amount in the ledger is recorded against an account combination: one value for each segment, joined with a dash. Each segment answers one question about the money.

Change any one segment and you get a different account combination with its own balance. Only the natural account is set up in the chart of accounts; the other segments are chosen on each journal line.

CCID: the ID behind the combination

The CCID (Code Combination ID) is the unique number the system gives an account combination the first time it is used. In FINOVA it is the Account Combination ID.

What you see

The account combination text, for example in the Account Combination column.

What the system stores

The CCID number. So the CCID is simply the internal code for the combination you see on screen.

i
In Oracle E-Business Suite this is called the CCID (table GL_CODE_COMBINATIONS). FINOVA keeps the same idea as the Account Combination ID in GL_ACCOUNT_COMBINATIONS.

Natural account, combination and CCID

The natural account is defined once in the chart of accounts, with its name and type. It can then appear in many combinations, and each combination has its own CCID and its own balance.

One natural account, many combinations

110101 is used by every branch, cost centre and business line that holds mortgage loans.

The natural account sets the behaviour

Every CCID built on 110101 behaves as an asset, whatever its other segments are.

i
The balance for one branch or business line is just the CCIDs that carry that branch or business line. CCID numbers and the ALX branch here are examples.

Posting to the ledger

To record a transaction you write a journal. Each line picks an account combination and a debit or credit. Nothing changes a balance until the journal is posted.

1EnterLines with account combinations and amounts
2SubmitThe system validates the journal✓ combinations valid and enabled
✓ debits = credits
3ApproveReviewed, when the source needs approval
4PostEach line updates the balance of its CCID for the period

The reversal number is the original number with REV- in front, and the search finds every journal number that contains what you type. So both journals appear, each with its own debit and credit lines. To see only the reversal, search with REV-. A journal can be reversed only once, and only after it is posted.

Quick rules

Six rules that keep the ledger right.

Full combination on every lineEvery segment filled, and the combination valid and enabled.
Debits = creditsA journal must balance before it can be posted.
Only posted journals countDraft, submitted and approved journals do not change balances.
Fix by reversing, never by editingReverse the posted journal, then post a correct one.
Balances live on the CCIDKept per CCID and period, then added up by any segment for reports.
A natural account has no balance of its ownIts balance is the total of every account combination that uses it. Money is held, and posted, only on combinations.
Segment
One part of an account combination, such as Company or Branch.
Natural account
The segment defined in the chart of accounts that says what the money is (110101).
Account combination
One value for every segment, joined in order: 01-HQ-1100-110101-MORTGAGE-000-000.
CCID
The unique ID of an account combination (Account Combination ID).
Posting
Applying an approved, balanced journal to the ledger balances.
Reversal
A new journal (REV-…) that swaps debits and credits to cancel a posted one.

Balances by period and currency

A CCID never changes with the period or the currency. Instead, one CCID holds many balance rows: one for each ledger + period + currency.
